Words this chapter explains

  • colonialismControl of another region by a country, including settlement and the imposition of its systems.
  • colonisersPeople or countries who take control of another region and benefit from that control.
  • civilising missionThe claim that colonisers were improving the people and places they ruled.
  • GDPA measure of the value of goods and services produced by a country in one year.
  • InquisitionA Roman Catholic Church tribunal that judged people suspected of heresy.
  • sepoyAn Indian soldier enrolled in the East India Company’s British Army.
  • Doctrine of LapseA rule allowing the British to annex states whose rulers died without a natural male heir.
  • princely stateAn Indian-ruled region that accepted British protection while keeping internal autonomy.
  • subsidiary allianceAn arrangement requiring Indian rulers to accept a British Resident and pay for British troops.
  • famineA period of severe hunger in which many people die.
  • revenueMoney collected by the colonial government, much of it through land taxes.
  • sannyasiA Hindu ascetic who traditionally travelled for pilgrimage and charity.
  • fakirA Muslim ascetic who traditionally travelled for pilgrimage and charity.
  • OrientalistA scholar of Sanskrit, Pali, Persian and other Asian languages and cultures.
  • British RajThe period of direct British Crown rule in India that began in 1858.
