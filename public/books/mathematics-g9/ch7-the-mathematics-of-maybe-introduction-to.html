Words this chapter explains

  • ProbabilityProbability measures how likely an event is.
  • Random eventA random event has known possible outcomes but no certain result in advance.
  • Subjective probabilityIt is a likelihood based on a person’s interpretation of evidence.
  • RandomnessRandomness is a situation in which you cannot predict exactly what will happen.
  • Random experimentA random experiment can be repeated, and its result may change each time.
  • Probability scaleThe probability scale runs from 0 for impossible to 1 for certain.
  • Experimental probabilityExperimental probability uses how often an event occurs during trials.
  • Theoretical probabilityTheoretical probability divides favourable outcomes by all possible outcomes.
  • Relative frequencyRelative frequency shows how often an event occurs compared with the total trials.
  • Statistical probabilityStatistical probability estimates likelihood from collected data.
  • EventAn event is one result or a group of results selected from a sample space.
  • Favourable outcomeA favourable outcome is one that matches the event being considered.
  • SamplingSampling means collecting evidence from a sample of a population.
  • Law of Large NumbersAs trials increase, experimental probability moves closer to theoretical probability.
  • Gambler’s FallacyIt is the mistaken belief that a random event becomes more likely after a run of opposite results.
