Words this chapter explains

  • anti-colonial movementA movement that opposes rule by a foreign colonial power.
  • satyagrahaGandhi’s method of truthful, non-violent resistance to injustice.
  • hartalA protest during which shops and other activities stop.
  • Rowlatt ActA 1919 law that gave the government sweeping powers to repress political activity.
  • civil disobedienceRefusing to obey colonial laws while opposing them non-violently.
  • KhilafatA movement to defend the Khalifa and his temporal powers.
  • swarajFreedom from British rule and government by Indians.
  • boycottThe refusal to deal with people, join activities or buy certain goods as a protest.
  • picketA protest in which people block the entrance to a shop, factory or office.
  • talukdarA landlord who collected rent and other charges from peasants.
  • begarUnpaid labour that villagers were forced to provide.
  • Purna SwarajThe demand for complete independence for India.
  • Simon CommissionA British commission set up to examine India’s constitutional system.
  • salt taxThe tax charged by the government on salt.
  • dalitPeople who were treated as oppressed because of the caste system.
  • separate electorateA voting system in which only members of one community choose representatives for that community.
