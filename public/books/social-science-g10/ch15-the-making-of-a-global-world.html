Words this chapter explains

  • cowriesShells used as a form of currency.
  • silk routesOverland and sea trade routes linking Asia with Europe and northern Africa.
  • Corn LawsLaws used by Britain’s government to restrict corn imports.
  • primary productsAgricultural goods and minerals sold in international trade.
  • regional specialisationDifferent regions concentrating on producing particular commodities.
  • refrigerated shipsShips that carry frozen food over long distances.
  • colonialismEuropean conquest and rule that pulled colonised societies into the world economy.
  • rinderpestA devastating disease that killed cattle and harmed people’s livelihoods.
  • indentured labourA contracted worker who serves an employer for a set time to pay for passage.
  • trade surplusA situation where a country’s exports are worth more than its imports.
  • mass productionProducing large quantities of goods quickly by repeating set tasks.
  • Great DepressionA worldwide collapse in production, employment, incomes and trade.
  • Bretton Woods institutionsThe IMF and World Bank, which managed parts of the post-war economic system.
  • exchange ratesRates that link national currencies for international trade.
  • MNCsLarge companies operating in several countries at the same time.
