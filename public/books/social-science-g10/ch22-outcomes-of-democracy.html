Words this chapter explains

  • dictatorshipA system of government in which rulers do not depend on citizens’ elected representatives.
  • normsAccepted rules or standards that guide behaviour and decisions.
  • transparencyOpenness that allows people to examine how government decisions are made.
  • accountabilityThe duty of government to answer to the people and face their questions.
  • responsivenessReadiness to pay attention to and meet citizens’ needs and expectations.
  • legitimacyThe quality of being accepted as rightful and supported by the people.
  • deliberationCareful discussion of different views before reaching a decision.
  • negotiationDiscussion in which people with different views try to reach an agreement.
  • economic developmentGrowth and improvement in a country’s economic conditions.
  • inequalityAn unfair difference in wealth, income or opportunities.
  • political equalityThe principle that every citizen has equal weight in political decisions.
  • majority ruleA system in which the larger group decides, provided that minority interests are respected.
  • social diversityThe different religions, languages, castes, regions and identities found within society.
  • dignityThe quality of being treated with respect and as a person of worth.
  • freedomThe ability to act and make choices without unwanted domination or control.
