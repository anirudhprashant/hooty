Words this chapter explains

  • MapA drawing of a place, as if you were looking down on it from above.
  • AtlasA book of maps.
  • Physical mapA map that shows natural things like mountains, oceans and rivers.
  • Political mapA map that shows countries or states, their boundaries and their cities.
  • Thematic mapA map that shows one special kind of information.
  • ScaleIt tells you how a short distance on the map matches a real distance on the ground.
  • Cardinal directionsThe four main directions: north, east, south and west.
  • Intermediate directionsThe directions in between the main ones: northeast, southeast, southwest and northwest.
  • SymbolA small sign or picture that stands for something real on a map, like a school or a river.
  • Survey of IndiaA government body that fixes the symbols used on maps of India.
  • SphereA round shape, like a ball.
  • GlobeA ball-shaped model of the Earth with a map drawn on it.
  • CoordinatesA pair of numbers or letters that tells you exactly where something is.
  • PoleOne of the two points at the very top and the very bottom of the Earth: the North Pole and the South Pole.
  • EquatorAn imaginary line around the middle of the Earth, halfway between the two poles.
  • LatitudeHow far a place is from the Equator, measured in degrees.
  • Parallel of latitudeAn imaginary circle around the Earth, running east to west, parallel to the Equator.
  • DegreeThe unit used to measure latitude and longitude. We write it with a small circle, like 30°.
  • ClimateThe usual weather of a place over many years.
  • TorridVery hot. Places near the Equator have a torrid climate.
  • TemperateMild: not too hot and not too cold.
  • FrigidVery cold. Places near the poles have a frigid climate.
  • AxisThe imaginary line from pole to pole that the Earth spins around.
  • Meridian of longitudeAn imaginary half-circle that runs from the North Pole to the South Pole.
