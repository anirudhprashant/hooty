Words this chapter explains

  • corneaThe thin transparent front part of the eye through which light enters.
  • retinaThe light-sensitive screen at the back of the eye where the image forms.
  • pupilThe opening in the iris that controls how much light enters the eye.
  • irisThe dark muscular diaphragm that controls the size of the pupil.
  • crystalline lensThe flexible lens inside the eye that makes fine adjustments to focus.
  • accommodationThe ability of the eye lens to change its focal length.
  • near pointThe closest distance at which the eye can see clearly without strain.
  • far pointThe farthest distance at which the eye can see clearly.
  • cataractA condition where the crystalline lens becomes milky and cloudy, causing vision loss.
  • myopiaA defect where nearby objects are clear but distant objects are blurred.
  • hypermetropiaA defect where distant objects are clear but nearby objects are blurred.
  • presbyopiaAn age-related defect where the eye loses its power of accommodation.
  • bi-focal lensesLenses with two parts, one for distant vision and one for near vision.
  • angle of deviationThe angle by which the emergent ray bends from the incident ray's direction.
  • dispersionThe splitting of white light into its component colours.
  • spectrumThe band of coloured components of a light beam.
