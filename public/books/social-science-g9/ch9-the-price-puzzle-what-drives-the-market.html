Words this chapter explains

  • DemandA product that buyers want and have the ability to purchase.
  • Purchasing powerThe ability to buy goods and services with the money available.
  • Law of DemandThe rule that quantity demanded falls when price rises and rises when price falls.
  • Demand curveA graph that represents a demand schedule.
  • Substitute goodsGoods that can replace one another, such as tea and coffee.
  • Complementary goodsGoods that consumers generally use together.
  • Diminishing marginal utilityThe principle that the extra usefulness of a product falls as more is consumed.
  • Law of supplyThe rule that quantity supplied rises when price rises and falls when price falls.
  • Market equilibriumThe point where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
  • Excess demandA situation where quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied.
  • Price ceilingA maximum price that sellers may charge for a product or service.
  • Price floorA limit below which the price of a product or service cannot fall.
  • MonopolyA market with one seller controlling the supply of a product or service.
  • Public goodsGoods and services provided by the government for everyone's benefit.
