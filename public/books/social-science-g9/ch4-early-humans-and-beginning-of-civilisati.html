Words this chapter explains

  • ArchaeologyThe study of early human sites and material remains to reconstruct the past.
  • ArtefactsObjects made by humans that help archaeologists study earlier ways of life.
  • Biological evolutionGradual physical and genetic changes through which early ancestors became modern humans.
  • Cultural evolutionThe way humans adapt to their surroundings through tools, technology and changing ways of life.
  • AustralopithecinesEarly human ancestors whose name means southern primates.
  • Homo sapiensThe scientific name for modern human beings.
  • HomininsA group including modern humans and early human-like ancestors.
  • FossilTraces or impressions from the distant past that have hardened into stone.
  • PalaeolithicThe old Stone Age, marked by hunting, gathering and simple stone tools.
  • MesolithicA phase using tiny stone tools between hunting-gathering and agriculture.
  • NeolithicThe food-producing period that included animal domestication and settled villages.
  • DomesticationBringing selected animals and plants under human control.
  • ChalcolithicA period using copper tools together with stone tools.
  • ZigguratA stepped temple whose upper part was a holy place.
  • City-stateAn independent state centred on a city and its surrounding territories.
